Assessing the Adaptive Capacity of Green Infrastructure to Climate Change Impacts on Water Quantity in Urban South African Environments

University of the Witwatersrand · Johannesburg focus

Background

Urban resilience through green infrastructure

The research evaluates how green infrastructure can respond to climate-driven changes in urban water quantity, flood behaviour and environmental performance in Johannesburg.

Study AreaCity of Johannesburg
Research ApproachMixed Methods
StatusResearch submitted · Outcome pending
Objectives

Research Focus

Performance

Evaluate green infrastructure effectiveness for stormwater, flooding and urban temperature regulation.

Implementation

Identify barriers and enabling conditions affecting green infrastructure adoption and long-term performance.

Integration

Develop guidance for incorporating green infrastructure and SuDS into urban water-management practice.

Methodology

Integrated spatial, hydraulic and stakeholder analysis

Remote Sensing & GIS

Sentinel-2 and Landsat 8 surface reflectance imagery, NDVI, NDWI, land-surface temperature and SRTM elevation analysis.

Hydrology & Flood Modelling

CHIRPS rainfall analysis, annual maxima, Gumbel EV1, Rational Method runoff estimation and HEC-RAS 1D steady-flow modelling.

Stakeholder Evidence

Interviews and surveys analysed alongside spatial and hydraulic evidence to capture implementation barriers, perceptions and enabling factors.

Research Technology Stack

Study Corridors

Johannesburg green infrastructure

The quantitative assessment focuses on the Braamfontein Spruit Wetland Restoration and the Jukskei River Revitalisation corridors, considering pre- and post-intervention environmental and hydrological conditions.
